pg 17:

Threats Targeting Virtual Servers 
    - Theft and misuse of private SSH keys
    - Attacks against unpatched, misconfigured, and vulnerable services listing on standard ports (FTP, SSH, HTTP, etc.)
    - Account hijacking (weak passwords, default passwords, security bugs, etc.)
    - Attack against systems that are not protected by a firewall
    - Deploy trojans or rootkits that are embedded in the guest OS images
A few mitigation techniques
    - Use host-based IDS such as OSSEC or Samhain and use a host firewall only opens required ports
    (firewall and security group)
    - Enable system auditing and event logging
    - Use secure passwords and change them frequently- Safeguard your SSH private key files
    - Harden VM images and always check the image's checksum to make sure it's not tampered
    - Require passwords for sudo * or role-based access (e.g., Solaris, SELinux)

Threat Modeling and Risk Assessment

- Method, Opportunity and Motive 
(systematic identification of security problems)
- Threat modeling is identifying security problems and potential threats
- in a systematic way to develop requirements, test plans and testing tools
    - Who might maliciously target our system? Identify internal and external attackers/threats
    - What assets can be targeted and to what extend
    - Rank threats based on their impact and criticality
- What suitable tools and strategies we can leverage to mitigate those threats?
    - e.g., encryption, firewall and intrusion detection systems, authentication, etc.
- Three common approaches for threat modeling: 
    - attack focused, 
    - asset focused, 
    - design focused
- Threat models can be represented UML diagrams, attack trees, and several other formats

pg 35:

Protecting Data at Rest
- Use end-to-end encryption to make sure data is encrypted at source and stored securely at the destination
    - Symmetric encryption technologies such as AES, Triple DES, etc. with secure keys (256-bits and more) 
    - Asymmetric encryption technologies
    - Public key infrastructure (PKI)
- In June 2009, IBM announced that they have found way to process encrypted data without decrypting it first (homomorphic encryption)
    - Requires immense computational capacity 
- Data integrity protection and verification (hash-based checksums, CRC codes, message authentication codes (MAC), manual verification, etc.)  


pg 36:

Data Remanence Steps

Data Remanence: residual representation of data that has been in some way nominally erased or removed
    - Residue might be left because of unsuccessful delete operation or be a result of storage device's physical properties
    - May result in inadvertent disclosure of sensitive information
- Data Clearing and Sanitization
    - Eradicating the data on media before reusing the media
    - DoD 5220.22-M Standard


pg 38:

Disaster Recovery and System Backups

- Need failover sites in case of natural or man-made disasters. Primary and disaster recovery data center
- Disaster recovery tested regularly, e.g. a minimum of twice yearly
- No service degradation; disaster recovery sites should be full duplicates of the primary sites
- Full system backups provide additional assurance
- System backups encrypted with AES 256 bit
- System backups kept off site for 30 days
- Apply the 3-2-1 rules: 3 copies, 2 different medium, 1 offsite

Key Takeaways
- Security has a broad perimeter and to mitigate threats, first step is identifying and assessing all attack targets, then performing thorough risk assessment and documenting mitigation procedures
- Clouds are not only accessed and attacked from the outside, but malicious insiders can be equally (or even more) harmful
- Cloud layers and services (IaaS, PaaS, SaaS) and different cloud types (public, private, virtual, hybrid) have their own unique security requirements and failing to secure one can potentially make the whole system unsecure
