Defining Machine Learning
Unlike Data Science, the definition for Machine Learning
is better understood and more agreed upon:
Machine Learning is concerned with the development of
algorithms and techniques that allow computers to learn.
- concerned with building computational artifacts,
i.e., computer programs that can learn, oftentimes
with computational output
- but the underlying theory is statistics

SLIDE 44
========================================================================
Data Science: Hype vs Reality
The Hype
-
Throw data at an algorithm, it
finds the answer
-
More data is always better
-
Models are objective and
unbiased
-
DS predicts the future reliably
-
It replaces domain experts
The Reality
-
Garbage in ->garbage out; ~60-
80% of work is cleaning
-
Quality beats quantity - noise
actively hurts
-
Models inherit the biases in their
data
-
Correlation != causation; every
prediction has uncertainty
-
It augments experts - domain
knowledge decides what matters
DS is powerful, but not magic.

SLIDE 65

Our Standard Value Chain: Parts of a Data
Science Project
- Collection: getting the data;
- Wrangling: data preprocessing, cleaning;
- Analysis: discovery (learning, visualisation, etc.);
- Presentation: arguing the case that the results are significant
and useful;
- Engineering: storage and computational resources across full
lifecycle;
- Governance: overall management of data across full lifecycle;
- Operationalisation: putting the results to work, so as to gain
benefits or value.
from Doing Data Science by Schutt and O'Neil, 2013 (available digitally through library)

Different roles: Neflix recommendation
Role
Description
Product manager
"Let's add a personalised recommendation row to the
home screen. Goal: increase watch time." Decides what
and why
Data analyst
"Users drop off after 3 titles, but people who watch
